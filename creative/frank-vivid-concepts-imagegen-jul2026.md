Ten speculative static concepts built on the Frank avatar, weighted toward
the pain points most likely to deliver high CTR and low ultimate CAC:
**#3 gets four slots** (best delivery, cheapest CPC, strongest emotional
driver), **#16 gets three** (best CTR), **#20 gets two**, **#8 gets one**.
Each concept includes a copy-paste prompt for the image generator, written

## Frank (assumed)

- Male, 58, married ~30 years, kids grown or in college.
- Engineer / manager / small-business owner — data-comfortable, tracks his
  401(k) weekly, trusts numbers over reassurance.
- On a statin; annual physicals that end with "looks good, see you next
  year" — which he no longer finds comforting.
- Lost his father (heart attack, early 60s) and more recently a friend or
  colleague who "seemed fine."
- Watches health YouTube late at night; hasn't told his wife how much he
  worries. On Facebook daily.
- Deepest fear: the sudden exit — missing the retirement he saved for.

## Rules baked into every prompt

- First-person confession voice; the "True or False?" device on every image.
- No bodies, chests, hospitals, pills, funerals, doctors, or before/after —
  objects and typography carry the story.
- No AI-generated likeness of the narrator, ever. The one face concept (the
  Torn Block) renders a **placeholder reveal** for compositing a real
  channel still.
- Campaign block colors where continuity helps: spring green ≈ #33E08C,
  azure blue ≈ #2BA3DE.
- All on-image text rendered exactly as quoted, spelled perfectly.

**On-image text:** `HE SEEMED FINE.` / `True or False? — I've had someone

**COPY-PASTE PROMPT:**
```

bottom center, small and understated, render exactly: "Watch the video".
Wide margins, generous negative space, strong hierarchy, flat matte
finish, no gradients, no glow, no imagery of any kind. All text spelled
exactly as written, crisp and legible at mobile size. Clean direct-
response editorial poster style.
```

## Why the weighting looks like this (CTR → CAC logic)

- **#3 (4 slots):** cheapest clicks in the account ($0.39 CPC, $18.63 CPM)
  and the deepest identification — grief-driven clicks are high-intent
  "this is me" clicks, which is what ultimately compresses CAC. Runs in its
  own isolated campaign per the standing caution.
- **#16 (3 slots):** highest CTR of the four (5.4%); reassurance fatigue is
  Frank's daily emotional weather, so frequency tolerance should be high.
- **#20 (2 slots):** solid CTR (5.1%) but the highest CPM — two concepts,
  scaled only if cost compresses.
- **#8 (1 slot):** clicks fine but is the most rational driver; rational
  clicks tend to be cheaper to win and weaker to convert, so it gets one
  strong swing (the Dashboard) rather than volume.

## Trafficking notes

- Every image ships with the standing structure: #3 concepts in the
  isolated campaign; #16/#20/#8 in the main test campaign against their
  block controls; success bar unchanged (beat the pain point's own block
  control at ≥300 impressions, kill under 3% CTR).
- Concept 3 (Torn Block) requires the real-photo composite step before
  upload — never traffic the placeholder version.
- Verify rendered text character-for-character before trafficking; image
  generators still misspell. Any misrender is a regenerate, not a
  touch-up-in-Canva, so the type stays clean.
