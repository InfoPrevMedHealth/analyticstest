first ~24 hours of the "Pain Points 7/16/2026" test (Jul 16–17 data, ~$48 total

identification-only ad layer, first-person voice end-to-end, narrator's face for

## Concept A — "I did everything right"

**Copy:** "I've done everything my doctor told me to do and still discovered
something was seriously wrong."

**Emotional driver:** Betrayal-by-compliance. This speaks to the "good patient"
— took the statin, kept the appointments, ate what they were told — who no
longer trusts that compliance equals safety. Identification is with the
*identity* (diligent, responsible) as much as the fear.

**Hook variants (ad layer, identification only):**
1. True or False? — "I've done everything my doctor told me to do and still
   discovered something was seriously wrong." *(control — current block)*
2. True or False? — "I followed every instruction I was given. I still got
   blindsided."
3. True or False? — "Being the 'good patient' didn't protect me."

**Primary text draft:**
"I kept every appointment. Took everything I was prescribed. Checked every box.
And I still found out something was seriously wrong. If you answered 'true,' I
made a video about the day I found out."

**Visual treatments:**
- V1: Current green/blue color block (control).
- V2: Color block top, narrator's face in the blue lower band, looking at
  camera. (Channel data says his face carries.)
- V3: 15-sec video — narrator reads the statement to camera, beat of silence,
  then: "That was true for me." Hard cut to card: "True or False?"

## Concept B — "The heart attack nobody saw coming"

**Copy:** "I've had someone close to me die from a heart attack I didn't see
coming."

**Emotional driver:** Witness grief converting into self-directed vigilance.
The click is "that happened to my brother / my dad / my friend — and now I
think about myself differently."

**Hook variants:**
1. True or False? — control block as shipped.
2. True or False? — "Someone I loved died of a heart attack that came out of
   nowhere."
3. True or False? — "The funeral changed how I think about my own health."

**Primary text draft:**
"He seemed fine. Everyone said he seemed fine. Since then I haven't been able
to stop wondering what 'seems fine' is worth. If that's true for you too, this
is the video I wish we'd both seen earlier."

**Visual treatments:**
- V1: Color block control.
- V2: Narrator face variant — somber, direct to camera. **No** funeral,
  hospital, or empty-chair imagery; that pairs confession copy with
  viewer-condition/sensitive-event imagery and invites a flag.
- V3: Video — narrator tells a 15-second version of losing his own colleague/
  patient (his real story, first person throughout).

**Compliance caution:** Death-of-a-loved-one copy is the most
sensitive-content-adjacent of the four. It is structurally safe (first person,
about the narrator's experience, not the viewer's condition), but consider
giving it its own campaign the way the three quarantined blocks are isolated,
so a reviewer misfire can't drag down the other three.

## Concept C — "I feel like I've been lied to"

**Copy (as shipped):** "I feel like I've been lied to about what keeps you
healthy."

**Emotional driver:** Betrayed trust in decades of mainstream health advice.
Broad resonance — this one likely over-indexes with the skeptic/researcher
segment that binge-watches the channel.

**⚠ Copy fix before scaling:** The shipped line mixes person — "I feel like
I've been lied to about what keeps **you** healthy." Recommend testing the
person-consistent variants below; the stray second person is exactly the
seam the personal-attributes filter reads for, and first-person consistency is
Prime Directive #2's whole point.

**Hook variants:**
1. True or False? — "I feel like I've been lied to about what keeps **me**
   healthy." *(minimal fix)*
2. True or False? — "I feel like I've been lied to about what keeps **us**
   healthy." *(collective variant)*
3. True or False? — "The health advice I trusted for thirty years let me down."

**Primary text draft:**
"Low fat. Whole grains. 'Your numbers look fine.' I believed all of it — until
I learned what it had been hiding. If you answered 'true,' I recorded what I
found."

**Framing guardrail:** Keep it as a *feeling* ("I feel like…"), never a claim
("doctors lie to you"). A stated feeling is identification; a stated claim
about the medical establishment risks the health-misinformation reviewer path.

**Visual treatments:** color-block control; narrator-face variant with a wry /
disappointed expression rather than anger.

## Concept D — "Show me the data"

**Copy:** "I want to see the actual data about what's happening inside my
body."

**Emotional driver:** Agency, not fear. The analytical viewer — engineers,
executives, quantified-self types — who is done with reassurance and wants
numbers. Least emotional of the four, likely the cleanest compliance profile,
and a natural fit for the channel's whiteboard style.

**Hook variants:**
1. True or False? — control block as shipped.
2. True or False? — "I don't want reassurance. I want numbers."
3. True or False? — "'You're probably fine' stopped being good enough for me."

**Primary text draft:**
"I got tired of guessing. I wanted the actual measurements — not a shrug and a
'see you next year.' If you answered 'true,' this video is where I started."

**Visual treatments:**
- V1: Color block control.
- V2: Narrator at his whiteboard (native to the channel's format, reinforces
  the data promise without showing any outcome or body imagery).
- V3: Video — narrator: "I'm a doctor, and even I wasn't shown my own data."
  *(only if true to his story — verify before scripting.)*

**Guardrail reminder:** "See the data" flirts with implying a test/protocol
offer. Keep the ad layer at *wanting* the data (identification); the tests
themselves live in the video content, per Prime Directive #3.
