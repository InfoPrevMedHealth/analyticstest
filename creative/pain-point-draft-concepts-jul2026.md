# Pain Point Draft Creative Concepts — July 2026

Early draft concepts for the four pain points that earned development based on
the first ~24 hours of the "Pain Points 7/16/2026" test (Jul 16–17 data, ~$48
total spend, ~51 link clicks). All concepts follow the PRIME DIRECTIVES in
CLAUDE.md: identification-only ad layer, first-person voice end-to-end,
narrator's face for imagery, no outcome/timeline/reverse-cure-unclog language.

## Confirmed mapping and build order

| Priority | Ad # | Copy | Impressions | Link CTR | CPC | Est. clicks |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | #3 | "I've had someone close to me die from a heart attack I didn't see coming." | 270 | 4.8% | $0.39 | ~13 |
| 2 | #16 | "I want proof, not promises, about whether I'm actually safe." | 130 | 5.4% | $0.73 | ~7 |
| 3 | #8 | "I want to see the actual data about what's happening inside my body." | 149 | 4.7% | $0.86 | ~7 |
| 4 | #20 | "I've done everything my doctor told me to do and still discovered something was seriously wrong." | 118 | 5.1% | $0.81 | ~6 |

#3 is the priority build — most delivery, cheapest CPM ($18.63), and the
lowest CPC in the account. #16 has the best CTR of the four. #8 and #20 click
well but at ~$40 CPMs, so watch cost as they scale.

None of these four is one of the three quarantined confession blocks
(post-diagnosis limbo / "haven't told my wife" / chest-twinge hypervigilance),
so they can share campaigns — with one caution on #3 noted below.

## Concept 1 — #3: "The heart attack nobody saw coming"

**Copy:** "I've had someone close to me die from a heart attack I didn't see
coming."

**Emotional driver:** Witness grief converting into self-directed vigilance.
The click is "that happened to my brother / my dad / my friend — and now I
think about myself differently."

**Hook variants (ad layer, identification only):**
1. True or False? — control block as shipped.
2. True or False? — "Someone I loved died of a heart attack that came out of
   nowhere."
3. True or False? — "The funeral changed how I think about my own health."

**Primary text draft:**
"He seemed fine. Everyone said he seemed fine. Since then I haven't been able
to stop wondering what 'seems fine' is worth. If that's true for you too, this
is the video I wish we'd both seen earlier."

**Visual treatments:**
- V1: Current green/blue color block (control).
- V2: Narrator's face in the blue lower band — somber, direct to camera.
  **No** funeral, hospital, or empty-chair imagery; that pairs confession copy
  with viewer-condition/sensitive-event imagery and invites a flag.
- V3: 15-sec video — narrator tells a short version of losing his own
  colleague/patient (his real story, first person throughout).

**Compliance caution — now higher stakes:** Death-of-a-loved-one copy is the
most sensitive-content-adjacent of the four, and it is now confirmed as the
account's best performer. It is structurally safe (first person, the
narrator's experience, not the viewer's condition), but a reviewer misfire
here would take down the strongest asset. Recommend giving #3 its own campaign
the way the three quarantined blocks are isolated — protecting the winner is
worth the extra campaign.

## Concept 2 — #16: "Proof, not promises"

**Copy:** "I want proof, not promises, about whether I'm actually safe."

**Emotional driver:** Reassurance fatigue. This viewer has heard "you're fine,
see you next year" for a decade and no longer accepts it as an answer. More
emotional than #8's data-curiosity — the underlying question is "am I actually
safe?", which is fear wearing an analytical coat. Best CTR of the four (5.4%).

**Hook variants:**
1. True or False? — control block as shipped.
2. True or False? — "'You're fine' isn't proof. I wanted proof."
3. True or False? — "I'm done accepting 'don't worry about it' as an answer."

**Primary text draft:**
"Every checkup ended the same way: 'Looks good. See you next year.' Nobody
ever showed me proof. If you answered 'true,' this video is where I started
asking for it."

**Visual treatments:**
- V1: Color block control.
- V2: Narrator face variant — steady, level gaze; the tone is resolve, not
  fear.
- V3: Video — narrator: "I'm a doctor. And for years, even I settled for
  'you're probably fine.'" *(only if true to his story — verify before
  scripting.)*

**Differentiation note:** #16 and #8 are siblings and will converge into the
same ad if drafted casually. Keep #16 anchored on the *feeling* (tired of
being reassured) and #8 anchored on the *numbers* (wanting to see the
measurements). If both run in one campaign, exclusion audiences won't separate
them — the creative has to.

## Concept 3 — #8: "Show me the data"

**Copy:** "I want to see the actual data about what's happening inside my
body."

**Emotional driver:** Agency, not fear. The analytical viewer — engineers,
executives, quantified-self types — who wants measurements, not opinions.
Least emotional of the four, likely the cleanest compliance profile, and a
natural fit for the channel's whiteboard style.

**Hook variants:**
1. True or False? — control block as shipped.
2. True or False? — "I don't want reassurance. I want numbers."
3. True or False? — "Opinions are fine. I wanted measurements."

**Primary text draft:**
"I got tired of guessing. I wanted the actual measurements — not a shrug and a
'see you next year.' If you answered 'true,' this video is where I started."

**Visual treatments:**
- V1: Color block control.
- V2: Narrator at his whiteboard (native to the channel's format, reinforces
  the data promise without showing any outcome or body imagery).
- V3: Video — narrator flips a whiteboard from a question mark to a simple
  chart sketch while reading the statement. No values, no targets — the sketch
  is texture, not a claim.

**Guardrail reminder:** "See the data" flirts with implying a test/protocol
offer. Keep the ad layer at *wanting* the data (identification); the tests
themselves live in the video content, per Prime Directive #3.

## Concept 4 — #20: "I did everything right"

**Copy:** "I've done everything my doctor told me to do and still discovered
something was seriously wrong."

**Emotional driver:** Betrayal-by-compliance. This speaks to the "good
patient" — took the statin, kept the appointments, ate what they were told —
who no longer trusts that compliance equals safety. Identification is with the
*identity* (diligent, responsible) as much as the fear.

**Hook variants:**
1. True or False? — control block as shipped.
2. True or False? — "I followed every instruction I was given. I still got
   blindsided."
3. True or False? — "Being the 'good patient' didn't protect me."

**Primary text draft:**
"I kept every appointment. Took everything I was prescribed. Checked every
box. And I still found out something was seriously wrong. If you answered
'true,' I made a video about the day I found out."

**Visual treatments:**
- V1: Color block control.
- V2: Narrator face in the blue band, looking at camera.
- V3: 15-sec video — narrator reads the statement to camera, beat of silence,
  then: "That was true for me." Hard cut to card: "True or False?"

**Watch item:** #20 has the highest CPM of the four ($41.10). If cost doesn't
compress as spend scales, the face/video variants are the lever to try before
cutting it.

## Appendix — On hold: "I feel like I've been lied to"

**Copy (as shipped):** "I feel like I've been lied to about what keeps you
healthy."

This statement was in the earlier creative share but is **not** one of the
four confirmed Tier 1 ads, so it's parked here rather than built. If it comes
back into rotation, two notes carry over:

1. **Person fix:** The shipped line mixes person — "…what keeps **you**
   healthy." Test "keeps **me** healthy" or "keeps **us** healthy" instead;
   the stray second person is exactly the seam the personal-attributes filter
   reads for.
2. **Framing guardrail:** Keep it as a *feeling* ("I feel like…"), never a
   claim ("doctors lie to you"). A stated feeling is identification; a stated
   claim about the medical establishment risks the health-misinformation
   reviewer path.

Draft hooks if revived: "The health advice I trusted for thirty years let me
down." / "I feel like I've been lied to about what keeps me healthy."

## Rollout notes

1. Build order: #3 → #16 → #8 → #20, per the confirmed mapping above.
2. Isolate #3 in its own campaign (see Concept 1 caution) — it is both the
   best performer and the most sensitive-content-adjacent copy.
3. Ship V2 (narrator face) variants against the color-block controls in the
   same ad sets — the single biggest open question the current test can't
   answer is whether his face beats the block at the ad layer.
4. Keep #16 and #8 creatively differentiated (feeling vs. numbers) so the two
   "verification" angles don't collapse into one ad.
5. Landing pages stay in the same first-person voice as each ad (Prime
   Directive #2) — one page per pain point if feasible, so the confession the
   viewer clicked is the confession the page continues.
6. Keep #15- and #17-style angles (whatever copy those numbers carry) out of
   the next build; they had real delivery and didn't click.
